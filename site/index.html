EVerest OCPP lib: a formal study

AboutA study of EVerest, open-source software that runs electric-vehicle chargers, and of how it follows OCPP, the standard chargers use to talk to their network.

is a full software stack for running a charging station, maintained as a Linux Foundation Energy project. , the Open Charge Point Protocol, is the open standard a charger uses to talk to the software that manages its charging network. Version 1.6, the one checked here, lets the network send a charger schedules that limit how much power it gives, and the charger must combine them into one limit at each moment.

The study compared EVerest’s code for that combining with a , and found one difference: EVerest restarts a daily schedule one second early. That holds under the study’s of the standard and in its smallest example; the drift grows by a second for each since a schedule first started. The study rates it low severity.

Pick a question below to see a short answer and only the evidence behind it. Each piece of evidence carries one of five strengths: Proved, Tested, Decided by a person, Assumed or Not yet reached. Not yet reached also covers a proof that is not yet finished. The glossary says more about EVerest and OCPP, with links to their own documentation.

It is a worked example of the argument in Review Evidence, Not Code (opens in a new tab). By Harry Nicholls (opens in a new tab), who directed the study and built the tool used to run it.

FindingA daily schedule should restart at 11:00:00; in the study’s smallest example, EVerest restarts it at 10:59:59.

The schedule first started at 11:00:00 UTC on 14 September 2016. At 10:59:59 UTC on 13 August 2026 the reference program gives a limit of 6,543.6 W. EVerest has already restarted the schedule and gives 9,249.9 W.

EVerest counts elapsed UTC seconds, which include leap seconds. The reference program follows the signed clock-time reading. The physical impact is small; the difference is concrete and repeatable.

Why it happensWritten rules and test report · Tested, Decided by a person

The finding depends on how the standard is read. That reading is a stated premise, not a hidden one.

The signed interpretation is that a daily schedule restarts at the clock time in its startSchedule. The study records that the standard’s wording also admits another reading.

EVerest calculates recurrence using elapsed UTC seconds, which include leap seconds. The reference program follows the signed clock-time reading.

A diagnostic rerun shifts older forward by the leap seconds in between, and all 2,000 cases then pass. That changes the inputs to isolate the cause. It is not a fix to EVerest.

Proof

What is provedModel and proofs · Proved, Not yet reached

checks mathematical arguments about the model. This is not a proof of the C++ implementation.

  • The combined limit is never above the charger-wide limit.
  • It is never above the applicable transaction limit.
  • While a transaction's own schedule applies, changing default schedules does not change the limit.

Not yet reached: Adding a lower-priority schedule that does not apply leaves the limit unchanged (I4, the fourth model rule).

The proof check names the completed theorems and rejects dependencies on sorryAx, the placeholder Lean records for an unfinished proof, or on non-standard axioms. The fourth, unfinished statement is not counted as proved.

Where the owner decidedCorrespondence review · Decided by a person

An AI searched for likely problems. Its verdict is not evidence; the owner’s recorded decisions are.

  • The owner approved interpretations A1 (a profile applies only while its schedule covers the moment) and A2 (a transaction profile overrules the default profiles only while its own schedule covers the moment) and reclassified two AI correspondence-review findings as abstractions rather than approximations.
  • The owner confirmed restoring the missing s ≤ t condition (the moment t cannot come before the schedule's start s) in D4, the rule for when a schedule covers a moment, on 2026-10-27.
  • Q4, the conformance rule (EVerest's limit conforms when it is at or below the model's), treats lower limits as conforming; the owner chose to warn about lost charging. The report retains the classification caveat recorded here.

Why the study is built this wayStudy design · Decided by a person

Each choice comes with the boundary it leaves.

Separate the model from the production implementation
A Lean reference program lets the study state and prove model properties, then compare its answers with the existing C++ implementation. A proof about that model does not establish correspondence with all production behavior.
Check the moments where schedules change
The harness compares transition instants from either program and one second either side. That makes a one-second timing difference visible. The generator still has bounded profiles, periods and time windows; agreement does not cover other inputs.
Record interpretation decisions before judging differences
The owner resolved ambiguities in the signed spec and reviewed the correspondence findings against those decisions. A difference caused by a disputed reading of the standard must remain distinct from an implementation defect.
Publish the limits beside the result
The study keeps the unfinished fourth proof, trusted dependencies and classification inconsistency visible, with pinned replay inputs. The leap-second input shift is a diagnostic experiment, not an upstream repair.

Assumptions

What the result depends onTrusted base · Assumed

The is part of the claim, not a footnote to it.

  • Lean v4.28.0 and its standard axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound for the stated completed proofs.
  • The reviewed interpretation of OCPP 1.6 edition 2 and the Lean model's correspondence to it.
  • The C++ compiler, driver, copied installation/orchestration glue, two shim headers, Python harness, JSON adapter and model runner.
  • The operating system timezone database read by the driver; its leap-second table must contain exactly the three leap seconds since 2010 (2012-06-30, 2015-06-30, 2016-12-31).

The driver compiles unmodified EVerest scheduling code, but supplies its own installation and orchestration glue and two shim headers. Those are part of what the comparison trusts.

Limits

Where the result stopsWritten rules and generator bounds · Not yet reached

Matching these inputs does not establish all OCPP behavior. Each unreached behavior needs a model extension or an explicit assumption.

At most 2 charger-wide, 3 default and 2 transaction profiles, up to 4 periods per profile, 15-minute offsets, 24- or 48-hour windows in 2026, on connector 1. Absolute, daily and weekly schedules are generated. Each case samples transition instants from either program and one second either side.

Default profiles on connectors 0 and 1 together are not generated: the signed spec leaves their interaction unresolved. The recurrence wording also admits another interpretation; the study records it as note N4 for the Open Charge Alliance (OCA): once a leap second falls between a schedule's start and now, "every 24 hours, at the same time" can mean elapsed seconds or clock time.
