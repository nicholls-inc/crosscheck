# Spec: `max_digits` is a range requirement

Intent: `intent/2026-10-07-max-digits.md`. Governing roadmap item: TB-1. Task: TB-1.13.

The executable form of each rule is a test named after it: `test_digits_limit` and `test_narrow_to_digits` in `cgv/src/bounds.rs`, `test_max_digits_bounds` in `cgv/src/model_extractor.rs`, `test_pydantic_max_digits_bounds` and `test_unreadable_override_of_digits` in `cgv/src/dataclass_extractor.rs`, and the fixture `cgv/test_fixtures/tb_max_digits/`. `cgv/scripts/max_digits_oracle.py` checks MD-1 to MD-3 against the real libraries: it extracts a Django and a pydantic field for every `max_digits` from 1 to 6 and every `decimal_places` (or one the extractor cannot read), and validates a grid of values with Django's `DecimalValidator` and with pydantic. On Django 5.2.18 and pydantic 2.13.5 it makes 18,448 checks with no failure. It needs Django and pydantic, so no CI job runs it yet.

- **MD-1. The limit.** `digits_limit(m, d)` is `m` nines with the last `d` after the point (`10^(m-d) - 10^-d`). With no `d` it is `m` whole nines (`10^m - 1`). It is `None` when `m < 1`, `d < 0`, `d > m`, `m > 38` (beyond `i128`) or `d > 30` (beyond `Dec`'s scale).
- **MD-2. Django.** A `DecimalField` with a literal `max_digits` has a range requirement of `[-limit, limit]`, intersected with its `MinValueValidator` and `MaxValueValidator` bounds. No other field type gets one, even with a `max_digits` keyword.
- **MD-3. pydantic.** A data class field with a literal `max_digits` (in `Field(...)`, `Annotated[..., Field(...)]` or `condecimal(...)`) has the same range requirement, intersected with its `ge`, `gt`, `le` and `lt` bounds. A field whose requirements a transforming validator clears keeps none. A `max_digits` or `decimal_places` keyword that is not a literal makes that value unknown, even when an `Annotated` alias gave a literal one, because pydantic lets the later `Field` override the alias.
- **MD-4. Reads.** A read of such a field carries the range as a guarantee, as for any validator bound and as a `CharField` read carries its `max_length`. So copying a wider decimal field into a narrower one is an error, like the length error in the bench case `length-cross-model`, and so is adding two reads of a field and writing the sum back into it (`a.balance = a.balance + a.fee` gives `range ≤ 199999999.98` against `≤ 99999999.99` for `max_digits=10, decimal_places=2`). The guarantee is as trusted as the validator bounds: Django does not run `DecimalValidator` on `save()`, and a database that does not enforce `numeric(m, d)` (SQLite) can hold a wider value.
- **MD-5. Verdicts.** In `tb_max_digits`, the writes of `Decimal("123456.78")` and `Decimal("-1000")` into `Price.amount` and of `Decimal("1000.5")` into `Quote.amount` are errors, and the writes of `999.99` and `-999.99` are not. Every other fixture gives the errors and exit code in its `expected.json`, and the bench corpus gives the outcomes in `cgv/bench/baseline.json`.

**Known gaps, not rules.**
- The total-digit conjunct of a `max_digits` field without a readable `decimal_places` is not yet reached. pydantic's `Field(max_digits=5)` rejects `99998.5` (6 digits), and the range `±99999` admits it. The blocking property is that the extractor has no total-digit guarantee for a source: it knows a value's magnitude and its fractional digits, not its digit count. The open question is whether such a guarantee is worth deriving (TB-1.33). With a readable `decimal_places`, the precision and range requirements together are the `decimalFieldAccepts` predicate, apart from the two gaps below.
- Zero written as `Decimal("0")` or `Decimal(0)` into a field with `max_digits == decimal_places` is accepted by the range, and Django and pydantic both reject it: they count the zero as one whole digit. The blocking property is that a range bound cannot exclude one interior value. The open question is whether that quirk is worth a check of its own. `Decimal("0.00")` is accepted by both libraries.
- A `max_digits` above 38 or a `decimal_places` above 30 gives no range requirement, so integer-digit overflow of such a field is not yet reached. The blocking property is the size of `Dec` (an `i128` mantissa, scale at most 30). The open question is whether any real schema needs a wider bound.
- A write of a value whose magnitude the extractor cannot bound gives the range warning (`range (unspecified)`), not an error, as for every range requirement. The message says `range ≤ 999.99` and does not name `max_digits`. TB-1.34 measures the errors MD-4 adds on a real codebase and decides on the message.
- No theorem links the range requirement to `decimalFieldAccepts` yet. TB-1.16 proves the range kind against `BehaviorModel.lean`, and TB-1.18 proves the precision kind together with it.
