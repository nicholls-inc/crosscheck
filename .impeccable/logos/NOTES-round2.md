## Selection before drawing

Choose concepts 1–5, respectively marks 6–10. They use five constructions: opposing cantilevers, a perforated disc, a vertical inference assembly, a receiving jaw and specimen, and an interrupted diagonal crossing. None reconstructs mark 4's diagonally divided square.

- **6 · Counterproof:** the most direct fusion of formal proof and a second independent account; the unequal arm heights preserve the possibility of disagreement. Avoid reducing the gap to a decorative seam. Risk: opposed turnstiles can read as an abstract H.
- **7 · Punched witness:** the circular, pierced ink mass adds a different typographic rhythm and makes the record physically inspectable. The displaced apertures hold a discrepancy without linework. Risk: two holes may suggest a button or a die; perforation alone does not communicate formal proof.
- **8 · Inference:** the authentic premise/rule/conclusion relationship earns its geometry; two premises remain visibly separate. Risk: inference notation is familiar, and a single conclusion can imply that comparison necessarily agrees.
- **9 · Gauge:** the independent object and the explicit clearance make checking relative to a named reference concrete. Risk: its reference jaw can resemble an E or a mechanical fitting.
- **10 · Split tally:** crossed witnesses make a compact X-shaped silhouette, but the interruption lets the viewer see the crossing instead of hiding one account. Risk: an X can mean rejection, and crossed bands are a familiar identity construction.

Concept 17 scores well but repeats the opposed-block strategy of 6. Concept 11 is too close to mark 4's split-solid idea. Concept 12 risks crop-mark familiarity. The selected five trade some score for actual construction diversity.

## Render inspection and revisions

Rendered all six marks, including the unchanged control, at **24px and 160px** using ImageMagick. `render/final-specimens.png` presents the actual pixels without enlarging the 24px specimens: left to right, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10; 160px above and 24px below. Also inspected the Chrome-rendered sheet at 1200px desktop and an emulated 390px viewport, including the inverse lockups and all six study placements. The final screenshots are `render/desktop.png` and `render/mobile.png`; three sequential mobile crops retain readable inspection detail.

The first pass changed:

- **6:** the original two-unit central interval looked too much like a slit closing an H at small size. Retracted each arm by half a unit, opening a **3-unit gap**, while retaining the three-unit difference in arm height. The independence is clearer in both polarities; the H association remains an honest risk.
- **10:** the first crossing had short interrupted arms and wide clearances, making it read too readily as a percentage sign at 24px. Lengthened both broken arms by 1.5 units in each axis; the perpendicular clearance is now about **2.1 units** rather than 4.2. It reads more as an interrupted crossing. The percent association is reduced, not eliminated, and is named on the sheet.
- **7, 8, 9:** retained their geometry after inspection. The six-unit square perforations in 7 remain open; the three-unit gaps above and below 8's inference rule remain distinct; 9's three-unit upper/lower clearances and four-unit inner clearance remain legible. No small detail had to be removed or replaced by colour.
- **Sheet verification:** a first narrow-window capture was cropped by Chrome's minimum desktop window width, so it was rejected as viewport evidence. Recaptured with explicit device metrics: `innerWidth` and document width both equal 390px. This corrected the capture, not the page. Final desktop width is 1200px with document width 1200px. Every specimen measures exactly 24, 64 or 160 CSS pixels; all study marks are 22px.

The final confirmation shows no filled-in apertures or merging components at 24px. Diagonals and circles naturally antialias; the rectilinear marks retain crisp substantial components. This is a visual inspection, not a guarantee of semantic recognition by an unfamiliar audience.

## Recommendation

**Choose 6, Counterproof.** Its two independently grounded proof supports are specific to Crosscheck's mechanism, and their displaced arms refuse to erase disagreement. It is substantial enough to stand beside mark 4, yet uses neither a divided square nor a thin schematic. At 22–24px the relationship survives in one colour. Its H association is the main weakness; use the Crosscheck lockup while the mark gains recognition.

**Runner-up: 7, Punched witness.** It has the most different silhouette in this round and a convincing relation to an inspectable record. Its square apertures remain clear in inversion. Choose it if a notarial, archival identity matters more than an immediate formal-logic reference; the button/die association is the cost.

8 is the clearest formal notation but the least proprietary. 9 offers the most concrete calibration metaphor but can become an E. 10 is the most visibly crossed form, with the strongest punctuation/percentage and rejection risks. None should replace explicit proved, tested, human-decided, assumed and not-yet-reached labels; none encodes a universal approval.
